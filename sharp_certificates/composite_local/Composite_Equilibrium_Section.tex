\subsection{A verified local equilibrium on the composite auction grid}
\label{sec:composite-dynamic-grid}
At endogenous share $\eta=0.45$, the dynamic first-price auction on the
21-belief-node, 60-time-step grid has exactly one equilibrium in an explicitly
specified neighborhood. The rounded finite-element (FEM) and neural (PINN)
paths, sampled at the 60 grid times, both belong to this neighborhood.
A separate calculation for the continuous-time, continuous-belief response
bounds the residuals of their published paths by $0.062785$ in total variation
(TV). Existence for the continuous composite follows from the previously
established Schauder argument. Its local and global uniqueness remain open.

The composite uses the iPinYou four-point background bid law
$(0.4023,0.2553,0.0198,0.3226)$ on $(0,.05,.10,.15)$, the fitted score
scale $.0110250693063193$, and the earlier Criteo-based regime specification.
Its offsets are approximately $(.071211,-.071211)$ and the prior probability
of regime one is $.4999294686139624$. The payoff array and exact decimals
are frozen in the replication files. The unit horizon, signal volatility $.2$,
opportunity rate $2.25$, five budget levels, feasible bids in $\{0,.05,.10\}$,
and Shannon temperature $.05$ match the archived model. The two data sources
supply separate inputs. The composite assumes a common structural game and
a monetary interpretation of the regime values. The equilibrium claims
are conditional on these choices.

\paragraph{Location of the grid equilibrium.}
Let $x$ record the first two bid shares, abstention and the low bid, at each
time. The high-bid share is $1-x_0-x_1$. Let $c$ be the rational center
archived as 120 integers divided by $10^{14}$ and set
$X_r=\{x:\|x-c\|_\infty\leq r\}$. On the primary grid, $X_{1/512}$
belongs to the product of probability simplices. The verified Lipschitz bound
for the response $\Phi_h$ on this cube is $0.996801$ and the center
defect is below $5.151\times10^{-15}$. A nested smaller cube gives
$\|x^*_h-c\|_\infty<6.31\times 10^{-15}$ for the same equilibrium.
The TV error of the center on this grid is below $1.262\times10^{-14}$.
The two sampled solver paths have maximum coordinate deviations from $c$
below $0.0016472$, compared with $1/512=0.001953125$.
Picard iteration under $\Phi_h$ from either sampled path converges to
this equilibrium.

\subsubsection{The interval proof for the dynamic grid}
For a belief $p$, event likelihoods $\ell_0,\ell_1$ conditional on regime,
and mesh nodes $k/(n-1)$, write $P=(1-p)\ell_0+p\ell_1$ and $J=p\ell_1$.
For a loss, use the conditional loss probabilities in place of the win
probabilities. If $J/P$ lies in cell $k$, the probability-weighted nodal
interpolation coefficients are
\[
 (k+1)P-(n-1)J,\qquad (n-1)J-kP.
\]
Both are affine in the two free coordinates of the rival bid law at that
time. Win and loss lead to their corresponding budget levels.
At $p=0,1$ the posterior stays at the endpoint. For interior beliefs an
interval sign test identifies all admissible cells. When the signs do not
select a single cell, the verifier takes the interval hull of every
admissible value and slope, including the zero slope at an inactive node.
This handles changes of interpolation cell inside the cube.

Let $K_a$ be the resulting jump kernel, $g_a$ the current expected net
reward, $j=\lambda\Delta t$, and $H=(1-j)I+\Delta t L_h$ the diffusion
and no-arrival matrix. Each diffusion rate to an adjacent interior belief
node is $[.2p(1-p)]^2/(2h_p^2)$, where $h_p=1/(n-1)$.
For the vector $V^+$ at the next time, set
$S_a=g_a+K_aV^+$ and $\pi_a=\exp(S_a/\varepsilon)/
\sum_{a'\text{ feasible}}\exp(S_{a'}/\varepsilon)$. Writing $m$ for
the current state masses, the discrete equations are
\begin{align*}
 V &= HV^+ + j\varepsilon\log\sum_{a\text{ feasible}}\exp(S_a/\varepsilon),\\
 m^+ &= H^\top m+j\sum_a K_a^\top(m\pi_a),\qquad
 (\Phi_h)_a=\sum_i m_i\pi_{ia}.
\end{align*}
Products inside the mass update are componentwise. The initial mass splits
the exact prior between its adjacent nodes at full budget, and $V_T=0$.
The mathematical scheme sums over feasible actions. The archived numerical
implementation gives infeasible actions a sentinel score that underflows to
zero in its binary64 softmax.

\paragraph{Verification.}
Differentiating the backward recursion gives
$dS_a=dg_a+(dK_a)V^++K_a\,dV^+$ and
$d\pi_a=(\pi_a/\varepsilon)(dS_a-\sum_k\pi_k dS_k)$.
Differentiating the forward equation propagates $dm$, $dK_a$, and
$d\pi_a$. For each cube the implementation encloses all entries of these
derivatives with ball arithmetic. The largest row sum of entrywise absolute
upper bounds majorizes the induced infinity norm. Along line segments the
map is Lipschitz and piecewise smooth. The cell formulas cover the
one-sided slopes, so the same bound applies across cell boundaries.
Writing $Y=\|\Phi_h(c)-c\|_\infty$ and $Z$ for this row-sum bound, the
verified inequalities $Z<1$ and $Y+Zr<r$ imply strict inclusion and
contraction. Banach's theorem yields local uniqueness and the error
bound $Y/(1-Z)$. This is also the identity-preconditioned test for
$F_h=I-\Phi_h$. The result leaves open the possibility of discrete
equilibria outside the certified cube.

\paragraph{Refinement.}
With the same exact inputs, the $11\times30$, $21\times60$, and
$31\times120$ grids have respective verified local gains below $0.182398$,
$0.183497$, and $0.183983$ at radius $2^{-28}$. Their centers lie within
$0.003293$, $0.001647$, and $0.000821$ TV of the FEM path at the
respective left-endpoint grid times. On the finest grid, a larger cube of radius
$2^{-10}$ also contains both sampled paths. Its gain bound is $0.678686$
with 59 possible interpolation crossings among 13,920 events.
A separate 75-decimal-digit interval
evaluation using posterior quotients verified the primary center defect.
Central differences of the archived solver checked all 120 derivative
columns, with largest entrywise discrepancies below $9.5\times10^{-10}$
at the two tested increments.

\subsubsection{A continuous-time residual from backward test functions}
Let $\bar q$ be the archived 320-cell FEM midpoint flow and $h_{ba}(p,t)$
the Gibbs policy induced by the archived affine value witness with scores
frozen at each cell midpoint. For any terminal action $a$ and target time,
use a backward test $U_b(t,p)=(1-p)u_{b0}(t)+pu_{b1}(t)$, piecewise
linear in time. With $p_0(p)=1-p$, $p_1(p)=p$ and conditional win
probabilities $\ell_{sk}(t)$ under $\bar q$, its exact generator is
\[
 (\mathcal L^h U)_b
 =\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{\min(2,b)} h_{bk}(p,t)
     \sum_{s=0}^1 p_s(p)\ell_{sk}(t)
       [u_{b-k,s}(t)-u_{bs}(t)].
\]
Bayesian conditioning times the event probability gives this identity.
The martingale belief diffusion has zero drift on an affine test.
Thus the computation bounds the continuous law, including all posterior
jumps, without discretizing its forward density.

Partition the unit horizon into $G$ output blocks. For each block
and each action choose a separate test, constant in time throughout its
target block. Earlier coefficients approximate the backward equation.
They are proposed in binary64 and then rounded to integers at scale
$10^{12}$. For any target time $t$ in its block, Dynkin's identity gives
\begin{align*}
 |\mathbb E h_{B_ta}(P_t,t)-\bar q_a(t)| \leq {}&
 |U_4(0,p_{\rm prior})-\bar q_a(t)|
  +\sup_{b,p}|h_{ba}(p,t)-U_b(t,p)|\\
 &+\int_0^t\sup_{b,p}|\partial_s U_b(s,p)+\mathcal L^h U_b(s,p)|\,ds.
\end{align*}
For each target block the integral includes its entire length in the upper
bound. Within earlier blocks both endpoint coefficients are tested.
Conditional on fixed auction primitives and policy within each fine
time cell, the residual is affine in the time interpolation coefficient.
Interval enclosures cover all fine cells in each block and all beliefs
in each listed subinterval. The certificate takes the maximum over the three
action coordinates, which equals TV for a difference of two three-action
probabilities. The independently replayed Bellman comparison and
common-arrival coupling bound the output effect of replacing the surrogate
policy with the true optimal policy by $0.0396548571$ TV.

\subsubsection{Transfer to the reported paths and scope of the result}
For paths $q$ in a convex box where the continuous response has
unweighted TV Lipschitz upper bound $L$, the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \|\Phi(q)-q\|_{\infty,\mathrm{TV}}\leq
 \|\Phi(\bar q)-\bar q\|_{\infty,\mathrm{TV}}+
 (1+L)\|q-\bar q\|_{\infty,\mathrm{TV}} .
\]
The comparison covers continuous and piecewise-constant rival flows with
the same uniform bounds on the primitives. We used the earlier positive
backward and forward comparison on two constant coordinate boxes.
They enclose, respectively, the FEM path with uniform TV margins of
$.001$ and $.01$. Integer rounding of the box faces enlarges them.
The unweighted gains are below $2.151217$ and $2.177967$.
Exact rational interpolation checks membership of the respective paths
and measures their distances to the midpoint candidate, including both
traces at step boundaries. For a discrete root, we linearly interpolate
the grid ordinates and extend the final ordinate constantly to time one.
We also include the verified TV uncertainty of the root.

\paragraph{Where uniqueness remains open.}
The tighter weighted full-path comparison on the $.001$ band returned
$2.065189$ after upward rounding, and the $.01$ band gave $2.089282$.
Both exceed one. The earlier composite existence proof uses an
instantaneous gain below $0.667786$, with the continuation and population
held fixed during this part of the argument. Over the full horizon,
perturbations also change values, filtering, and the distribution of
budgets. We have no verified enclosure of the derivative on all time
and belief modes that would transfer the grid uniqueness result.
The residual bounds above give no certified distance to a particular
continuous composite equilibrium. Failure of the gain test is compatible
with a unique equilibrium.

\paragraph{Allocation-only comparison and reproducibility.}
For the distinct iPinYou allocation calibration at $\eta=.45$ with
benchmark regime rewards, we recomputed the continuous comparison,
the two Bellman and forward residuals, and the associated radii.
The FEM radius was $0.0517736002$ and the PINN radius was $0.0517471716$,
each relative to its unique continuous equilibrium. Both radius files
matched the archived copies byte for byte. An integer-only replay also
gave iPinYou unweighted bounds of $0.4676772289$ at $.30$ and
$0.7900322858$ at $.45$. These are computational checks carried out
within this study. An external review of the proofs and implementation
remains outstanding. The auction logs do not identify private valuations,
advertising incrementality in the composite game, or whether bidders
played any computed equilibrium.
